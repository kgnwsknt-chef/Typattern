The QWERTY keyboard layout was designed in the eighteen seventies for
early typewriters, long before computers existed. A popular story
claims that the letters were arranged to slow typists down, but a
more likely reason is mechanical. Early typewriters jammed when
neighboring type bars were struck in quick succession, so common
letter pairs were spread apart to keep the machine running smoothly.
Many alternative layouts have since been proposed, and some may allow
faster typing. Even so, QWERTY has survived for more than a century,
mostly because millions of people already know it and switching would
mean learning to type all over again.
